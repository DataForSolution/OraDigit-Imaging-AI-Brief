# OraDigit Imaging AI Weekly Brief — September 14, 2026

**Status:** Published research note  
**Coverage focus:** Baseline September imaging-AI landscape  
**Prepared for:** OraDigit

## Executive signal

The strongest signal entering mid-September is **market maturation rather than one single product announcement**. FDA's September update states that more than 1,600 AI-enabled medical devices have been authorized in the United States. Radiology remains a major center of that activity. For OraDigit, "AI for imaging" is no longer sufficient differentiation; the opportunity shifts toward workflow integration, modality expertise, quantitative reliability, reimbursement-aware decision support and longitudinal analytics.

## 1. Imaging AI is becoming infrastructure

**Verified development:** FDA updated its AI-enabled medical-device resources on Sept. 4, 2026 and reports more than 1,600 authorized AI-enabled devices as of September 2026.

**Why it matters:** Buyers now face a second-order problem: deciding which algorithms belong in which workflow, how results should be routed, how performance should be monitored and how staff should respond when outputs conflict with clinical judgment.

**OraDigit implication:** emphasize workflow and governance layers around imaging AI rather than attempting to compete with every detection model.

Source: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices

## 2. CCTA shows what reimbursement-aware imaging AI looks like

**Verified current policy:** CMS local coverage policies describe circumstances in which AI-enabled quantitative coronary topography/coronary plaque analysis using CCTA may be considered reasonable and medically necessary. The base CCTA must already be completed and interpreted, software must meet regulatory requirements, and coverage is limited to defined clinical scenarios.

**Why it matters:** This connects patient selection, acquisition quality, radiologist interpretation, post-processing, documentation and reimbursement.

**OraDigit implication:** cardiac CT software should be designed around the complete workflow. A practical product could verify protocol readiness, acquisition quality, required documentation and downstream eligibility before advanced quantitative analysis.

## 3. PET/CT: quantitative software is becoming more important

PET increasingly depends on reproducible quantification rather than visual interpretation alone. Brain PET adds tracer-specific quantitative frameworks, while oncology and theranostics demand lesion-level and longitudinal consistency.

Potential value areas:
- harmonized measurements;
- protocol and uptake-time QA;
- automated registration;
- longitudinal comparison;
- response analytics;
- structured reporting.

## 4. Other modalities: AI is spreading across the imaging stack

FDA's device inventory includes AI-enabled products spanning CT, MR, ultrasound, mammography and image processing.

**Implication:** OraDigit should maintain a modality-agnostic platform architecture while using modality-specific rules. Cardiac CT QA, brain PET quantitation and breast-imaging triage may share infrastructure but not clinical logic.

## Strategic takeaways

- **Build:** workflow intelligence, QA, quantitative analytics and decision-support infrastructure.
- **Partner:** with validated modality-specific algorithms rather than rebuilding every detector.
- **Monitor:** reimbursement pathways that turn software outputs into sustainable services.
- **Avoid:** treating authorization count as evidence of better patient outcomes.

## Evidence gap

The FDA device count measures regulatory authorization, not deployment, clinical utility or return on investment. Adoption must be tracked separately.
