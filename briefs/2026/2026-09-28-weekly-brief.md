# OraDigit Imaging AI Weekly Brief — September 28, 2026

**Status:** Published research note  
**Coverage window:** September 21–28, 2026  
**Prepared for:** OraDigit

## Executive signal

The week reinforces a shift from isolated algorithms toward **regulated workflow ecosystems**. FDA's AI-device resources describe more than 1,600 authorized AI-enabled devices, current CT triage products are expanding the findings handled inside one workflow, cardiac CT has a defined coverage pathway for certain quantitative post-processing, and radiotherapy vendors are emphasizing automation across the treatment journey. For OraDigit, the strategic opportunity sits between acquisition, interpretation and action.

## 1. CT triage expands beyond single findings

**Verified regulatory fact:** FDA 510(k) K260906 for a2z-Abdo-Triage has a Sept. 16, 2026 decision date and is classified as radiological computer-assisted triage and notification software.

The vendor announced Sept. 21 that the clearance expands its abdomen-pelvis CT triage capability.

**Why it matters:** when one product handles more findings, departments must define which alerts are urgent, who receives them, how duplicates are handled, whether worklist priority changes and how errors are audited.

**OraDigit implication:** workflow orchestration and AI governance may become more valuable than adding another narrow detector.

## 2. PET/CT: neuro PET is becoming an analytics workflow

Recent PET developments form a coherent pattern:
- Tauklarify adds another FDA-approved tau PET tracer;
- NeuroQuant PET supports automated quantitative analysis;
- MICSI-PET received FDA clearance in September.

Brain PET increasingly resembles a measurement pipeline involving tracer selection, acquisition, registration, standardized quantification, interpretation and longitudinal follow-up.

**OraDigit implication:** a vendor-neutral PET workflow layer could manage metadata, quantitative comparability, prior-study alignment and structured reporting.

## 3. CCTA: reimbursement creates a product-design constraint

CMS coverage for AI-enabled quantitative coronary topography/plaque analysis is conditional. The base CCTA must be completed and interpreted first, and the clinical scenario must meet policy criteria.

A practical CCTA intelligence module could combine:
- readiness checklist;
- heart-rate/rhythm documentation;
- protocol selection;
- contrast and acquisition QA;
- structured CAD-RADS handoff;
- eligibility checks for advanced analysis;
- coding/documentation prompts.

Sources:
- https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/lcd.aspx?lcdid=39883&ver=8
- https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleid=59769

## 4. Therapy analytics: automation moves across the treatment journey

At ASTRO 2026, RaySearch highlighted automation in planning and oncology workflow through RayStation and RayCare.

**Evidence status:** Vendor-reported.

**Why it matters:** therapy software is moving from individual algorithms toward connected workflows linking imaging, segmentation, planning, replanning and outcome tracking.

Source: https://www.raysearchlabs.com/astro-2026/

## 5. Regulation and governance

FDA's September AI-device update reinforces total-product-lifecycle thinking. Imaging organizations increasingly need inventories of deployed models, intended uses, versions, validation status and performance monitoring.

**OraDigit opportunity:** a lightweight imaging-AI governance layer could connect device identity, regulatory status, deployment site, workflow role, version, validation metrics and incident/performance monitoring.

## Strategic takeaways

- **Build:** modality-aware workflow and quality intelligence.
- **Partner:** with established algorithm vendors where outputs can feed an OraDigit workflow layer.
- **Monitor:** PET quantitative standards, CCTA reimbursement and therapy automation.
- **Publish:** explainers that separate regulation, evidence and vendor claims.

## Evidence gaps

Public evidence remains inconsistent on real-world AI utilization, alert burden, time savings, downstream outcomes and total cost of ownership. Treat these as research questions rather than assumed benefits.
